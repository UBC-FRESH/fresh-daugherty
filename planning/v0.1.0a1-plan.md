# fresh-daugherty master plan — v0.1.0a1

Reproduce Daugherty (1991), *Credibility of Long Term Forest Planning:
Dynamic Inconsistency in Linear Programming Based Forest Planning Models*
(PhD thesis, UC Berkeley), as closely as possible using `ws3`, openly and
reproducibly, and back a short peer-reviewed paper that repeats the thesis's
premise -> model -> results -> conclusion with an open stack.

## 1. Context and motivation

Daugherty (1991) proves that LP-based forest-planning models solved as
**open-loop** formulations admit **dynamically inconsistent** plans: plans
that fail Bellman's principle of optimality — a future planner, re-solving
from the realized state under the same goals, would not follow the plan's
tail. Such plans are not a credible basis for policy, and trade-off /
shadow-price analysis derived from them is biased.

The thesis is effectively inaccessible (archival hard copy only) and was
never published peer-reviewed. The trap it documents is repeatedly
rediscovered (fresh-fuchs documents the exposure on its own test instance —
`fresh-fuchs/planning/dynamic-inconsistency-note.md`). This project makes the
result accessible, transparent, reproducible, and citable.

### Citable companion reference (Model I/II/III formulations)

The Model I / Model II / Model III formulations and their compare/contrast
are documented in an accessible, citable source (text-layer Springer
handbook chapter), which this project cites alongside the thesis:

  Gunn, E. A. (2007). *Models for Strategic Forest Management.* Chapter 16
  in A. Weintraub, C. Romero, T. Bjørndal & R. Epstein (eds.), *Handbook of
  Operations Research in Natural Resources*, International Series in
  Operations Research & Management Science, Springer, pp. 317-341.

Gunn (2007, ch. 16) describes the three model forms (Model I: per-analysis-
area prescriptions, simple constraints, many variables; Model II: stands
aggregated, network arcs = origin period i -> regeneration-harvest period j,
fewer variables but more/harder constraints; Model III: age-class aggregation
as a network flow) and the harvest-flow constraints (level flow 3a,
non-declining yield 3b, generalized 3c). Notably, p. 331-332 **cites
Daugherty (1991)** and describes the dynamic-inconsistency phenomenon
directly (the "declining non-declining yield": the open-loop model schedules
expensive late-horizon harvests at low discounted cost to prop up a high
initial harvest; once the initial harvest is taken, the late harvests are no
longer worthwhile). This is exactly the behaviour reproduced here.

### Key thesis content (the reproduction target)

From the thesis (scanned copy held locally; page numbers are the printed
thesis pages):

- **The forest-planning model** (ch. 3): a strata-based long-term timber
  harvest-scheduling LP. Daugherty analyses a **Model II** formulation
  (existing vs regenerated strata distinguished by separate decision
  variables) because it makes management intensification and rotation
  flexibility easier to analyse; the objective `Z` sums net revenue over
  existing (`x_smij`) and regenerated (`y_rqjk`) decision variables (eq. 3-1).
- **Open-loop vs consistent solutions** (ch. 3): open-loop LP lets the
  current planner precommit future planners to the derived schedule. A
  **consistent** (credible) solution is the recursive/subgame-perfect one in
  which, at every period, the remaining plan is re-optimal from the realized
  state (eq. 3-34).
- **Drivers of inconsistency** (ch. 3): the harvest-flow constraint (the
  explicit between-period link), disequilibrium forest structure, and
  negatively-valued strata. The interest-rate interaction is *not* the naive
  one: the discount-factor shift between periods is uniform and drops out of
  the consistency requirements (p.58-59).
- **Measurement** (ch. 5): an iterative LP simulation of sequential
  replanning; inconsistency measured as the changes in decisions and in
  projected output levels over time; disequilibrium magnitude/type/pattern;
  harvest-flow-constraint and management-intensity treatments.
- **Experiments** (ch. 5-6): occurrence and magnitude of inconsistency over a
  range of initial forest conditions, harvest policies (harvest-flow
  constraints), management intensities, timing choices, and interest rates.
- **Alternatives / remedies** (ch. 7): precommitment (generally non-credible
  except legal requirements such as regeneration mandates), sequential
  harvest-flow constraints (remove the explicit link but leave implicit
  supply links), generating consistent solutions.
- **Implications** (ch. 7): inconsistent plans are not credible as plans or
  as economic models; constraint-cost / shadow-price analysis from them is
  biased.

## 2. Approach and reuse boundary

- Build on `ws3` (`ForestModel`, LP machinery). ws3 currently asserts "Only
  Model I supported for now" — the Model II path (`_bld_p_m2`,
  `_gen_vars_m2`, `_cmp_cflw_m2`, `_cmp_cgen_m2`, `_cmp_sch_m2`) is stubbed.
  A faithful reproduction of the Model II analysis requires implementing
  Model II in ws3 (a reusable ws3 contribution, done as a ws3 feature branch
  + release).
- Do not re-implement ws3's LP machinery; extend it.
- Cross-reference `fresh-fuchs` for the real tsa29mini test instance and the
  CI-safe characterization test (`tests/test_dynamic_inconsistency.py`).

## 4. Validation anchors

To be populated in Phase 1/5 from the thesis scan: the thesis's base-case
schedule outputs (per-period harvest volumes / output levels) and the
inconsistency-occurrence results, each with a recorded tolerance and, for
any deviation, an explicit reason. The fresh-fuchs synthetic characterization
(`tests/test_dynamic_inconsistency.py`: ~9.5% tail divergence, discount-rate
invariant on that instance) is the cross-check anchor for the simulator.

## 5. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| Thesis data not fully recoverable from the scan | Reconstruct with documented assumptions; record every substitution; sensitivity-check |
| ws3 Model II is a large, subtle extension | Isolate in a ws3 feature branch with its own tests; reproduce Model I first so the thesis core lands even if Model II slips |
| The scan is the only source (no text layer) | OCR / manual transcription of the needed tables; record transcription provenance |
| Scope creep into "new science" | The paper repeats the thesis; new analysis is explicitly out of scope |

## 6. Explicit out of scope

- New science beyond Daugherty (1991).
- Any change to `fresh-fuchs` itself.
- Recourse/rolling-horizon as a "fix" (it changes the object of study).
