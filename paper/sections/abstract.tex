\begin{abstract}
Long-term timber harvest schedules produced by linear programming (LP) are routinely presented to decision-makers as credible statements about the future, and their shadow prices are used to value constraints, land, and policy trade-offs. But an open-loop LP solution is a plan made as if the planner could precommit to every future decision, while real planning is sequential: each period, the planner observes the realized forest state and re-solves. In a 1991 PhD thesis that was never peer-reviewed, P. J. Daugherty showed that LP forest plans solved open-loop admit \emph{dynamically inconsistent} plans---plans that fail Bellman's principle of optimality because a future planner re-solving from the realized state would not follow the plan's tail. We formalize dynamic inconsistency for LP forest plans with an operational divergence metric and provide the first open, fully reproducible quantitative benchmark of the phenomenon, reproducing and refining Daugherty's Umpqua National Forest FORPLAN case study in the open-source \texttt{ws3} wood-supply model (Paradis, 2026) from the public planning documents. Across the full experiment grid (18 initial forest conditions $\times$ 4 discount rates $\times$ 6 harvest-flow policies), dynamic inconsistency is pervasive under harvest-flow constraints (73\% of cells) yet largely absent without them at positive discount rates (the no-flow control is consistent at 4--6\%), confirming the flow constraint as the operative between-period link. Inconsistency occurs at every discount rate, its magnitude is rate-dependent, and the non-declining-yield policy is the most inconsistent. On the all-mature landbase, the open-loop non-declining-yield plan promises a level harvest flow that sequential replanning revises steadily downward---the ``declining non-declining yield.'' The model, data, and experiment are openly available for verification, citation, and extension.
\end{abstract}
